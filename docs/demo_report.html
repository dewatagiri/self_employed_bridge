Developmental pricing

DEVELOPMENTAL PRICING: this product is priced below a commercial return on purpose. The funders accept a planned loss in return for additionality.

The DFI and the guarantor knowingly price at or below cost to reach borrowers the market does not serve. This section shows that planned loss, counting every funder including the sponsor's reserve: it is the price, not a forecast. It assumes no additionality; whether the bridge reaches new-to-credit borrowers and unlocks jobs is measured in the pilot (gate 4), and the cost per additional borrower below is an illustration across shares, not a finding. The borrower's all-in charge is capped: financier fee + guarantee fee ≤ the cap, charged on the drawn amount, simple interest. Funders' loss budget per cohort: RM2,000.

The guarantee fee is above the all-in cap, so nothing is left for the financier. Choose a lower guarantee fee or a higher cap.

Risk and return: the DFI and the guarantor

Each party carries its own costs. The DFI pays interest on the debt that funds the loans, holds equity against them, and bears the cost to serve. The guarantor sets its cap aside as capital for the length of a cohort and pays an admin cost per bridge. The costs are demo placeholders, empty in the real template: the DFI and the guarantor must supply their own. Return on capital is compared with each party's required return, and the gap is the spread it earns. Risk is shown as a bad group, the average of the worst 5% of groups, and the chance of a loss-making group. The sponsor's reserve takes the first losses and is shown only for context.

The guarantee fee is above the all-in cap here, so nothing is left for the DFI.

The sponsor's reserve: what it costs and what it buys

The sponsor funds the first-loss reserve up front, into a ring-fenced account (a promise is not enough: the guarantor's cover sits on top of it). Losses use it first. What the cohort does not spend goes back to the sponsor once its loans are closed and recoveries are in, so the sponsor's cost is what is spent plus the cost of having the money tied up for the cohort's life. The sponsor earns no financial return: this is the grant that pays for additionality. The cost of money tied up uses the sponsor's own cost of funds (4.0% a year here, a placeholder). The other columns use the config's own cover, guarantee fee, all-in cap and cost to serve.

Damage rate and the loss trip wire

A trip wire pauses new loans when the loss so far, after recoveries, passes a line, counted as a share of the money lent on loans whose outcome is already known. It trades reach for loss: every loan not made is a borrower not reached, which is what developmental pricing is paying for. It also cannot stop loans already out: outcomes arrive about 10 loans late here, and the wire cannot fire before 20 loans have a known outcome. Damage is the two endings abandoned and rejected; late payments and cash to the painter keep the chances in the config. Each setting is run on the same random draws as the no-trip-wire case, so the loss avoided and loans given up are paired, not noise.

Damage, late and cash chances add to more than 100% for this setting.

Method and limits

  • One run is one sponsor cohort. Each job draws a materials cost and a deposit; the bridge amount uses the same rule as the live release decision, and stops at the cohort cap.
  • Each bridge draws one outcome. Loss = amount × (1 − recovery rate for that outcome).
  • Losses run through the layers in event order: the reserve is shared across the cohort, then the guarantor up to its cap, then the financier.
  • The guarantee fee is charged to the borrower on drawn amounts and passed to the guarantor.
  • The stop line uses the bypass rate to date, tested once the minimum number of bridges is out.
  • Not modelled: changes in behaviour from pricing or margin of advance, correlation between bridges, collection costs beyond cost to serve, timing of cash.
